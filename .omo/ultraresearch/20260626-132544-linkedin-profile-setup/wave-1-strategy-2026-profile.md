# Wave 1 Digest: 2026 Recruiter-Facing Profile Strategy

Worker: Rawls the 10th  
Access date: 2026-06-26

## Key Findings

- A technical student/early-career profile should act as a skills-indexed proof page: role keywords in headline, explicit skills throughout, evidence in Featured/projects.
- "Student at X" alone is weak because the headline appears in search results and should promote expertise and target role.
- LinkedIn Skills Match and recruiter skill filters make explicit skills plus implicit skills in headline/About/experience important.
- Skills-based hiring is increasingly used in screening and interviewing, and AI skills should be explicit when backed by projects.
- Featured should be proof/conversion after profile click, not search optimization.
- Open to Work can be all-members or recruiter-only; recruiter-only is more discreet but not a total privacy guarantee.
- Tone should be technical and evidence-backed, not inspirational or keyword-stuffed.

## Recommended Structure

- Headline: target roles + 3-5 recruiter search skills + proof/domain.
- About: what systems the user builds, target roles, recent proof, strongest skills, current learning gap, what roles/locations are desired.
- Featured: GitHub/profile, strongest project demo/readme, model card or public experiment surface, learning archive, optional strong post.
- Skills: exact terms from target job descriptions, only if defensible from project/coursework.

## EXPAND

- LEAD: Exact LinkedIn Recruiter ranking weights are not publicly disclosed — WHY: Public docs confirm fields and filters, but not scoring weight — ANGLE: interview recruiters or test with a paid Recruiter account.
- LEAD: Open to Work public-frame sentiment varies by industry and seniority — WHY: Official docs explain visibility, not market perception — ANGLE: compare recruiter commentary by tech, consulting, startups, and enterprise.
- LEAD: AI/software keyword taxonomy should be role-specific — WHY: AI Engineer, ML Engineer, Data Engineer, and SWE Intern surface different searches — ANGLE: collect 2026 job descriptions from target companies and extract recurring skills.
